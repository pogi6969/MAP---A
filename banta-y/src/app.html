1 · Network

Where people spend the day

Dengue mosquitoes bite by day, so what matters is where people are in the daytime, not only where they sleep. List the barangays, how many mosquitoes each holds, and how much of the day residents spend away. BANTA-Y turns this into the share of the biting hours each barangay's residents spend in every place.

Have a household-survey index instead of mosquito counts?

Enter an index for each barangay (for example a house index or a Breteau index from the larval survey) and one barangay where a mosquito count gave mosquitoes per resident. BANTA-Y assumes mosquitoes per resident are proportional to the index and scales the others. Check the result with your health office's entomology staff.

2 · Transmission

How far dengue can spread

The reproduction number R0 of the whole network is the number of new cases one case causes, through mosquitoes, at the start of an outbreak. Above 1, dengue can take hold. BANTA-Y also splits it into the share carried by each place.

Season

Rain and heat change mosquito numbers. The season factor multiplies every barangay's mosquitoes: 1 is the season of the survey, above 1 the peak of the rains.

Who has the cases, and where transmission happens

Cases are recorded by home address, but people are often infected where they spend the day.

    Transmission parameters

    Declared design values within published ranges for Aedes aegypti (Mordecai et al., 2017). Change them to test other assumptions.

    Barangay by barangay

    Local exposure number Ri: the value of R02 residents of barangay i would face from the mosquitoes they meet over their day. R02 always lies between the smallest and the largest Ri. Hotspot score: drop in R02 per team-day, times 1,000.

    5 · Early warning

    An alarm that adds up small rises

    A single week rarely stands out, but several weeks a little above normal add up. The Poisson CUSUM alarm sounds when the excess over the expected count has piled up to a threshold.

    Weekly cases

    One line per week: week, cases, expected cases in a normal year. Paste from a spreadsheet.

    Alarm settings

    Reference value kt = (θ − 1) μt ÷ ln θ. Defaults: h = 12.75 and z = 3.64 give both rules about the same chance of a false alarm in a normal year (0.046 and 0.050) for the synthetic municipality of the study (6 to 11 expected cases a week).

    6 · DOH 4S

    The 4S checklist

    The Department of Health's 4S strategy against dengue. Tick each one as your barangay does it; the ticks stay on this device.

      Source: Department of Health 4S strategy: Search and destroy breeding sites, Self-protection, Seek early consultation, Support fogging or spraying only in hotspots.

      BANTA-Y is a planning aid. It is not an official order and not medical advice. Anyone with fever should seek early consultation at a health center. Numbers from the test network D1 are synthetic.

      About BANTA-Y

      BANTA-Y (say BAN-tai, from bantay, to watch or to guard) is the app of the study Where People Go, Dengue Goes: A Verified Mobility-Coupled Network Model for Ranking Dengue Hotspots, Issuing Early Warnings, and Allocating Vector Control at Least Cost in Albay. Every computation runs on this device.

      Researchers: Brimbuela, Peter John A.; De Asis, Dave Justin Ll. (lead researcher and founder); Shin, Yeaseong. Research adviser: Calleja, Dick Anthony M. Legazpi City Science High School, Bitano, Legazpi City, Albay.
