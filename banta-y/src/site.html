Bantay dengue sa bawat barangay

BANTA-Y

Say BAN-tai, from bantay: to watch, to guard.

Where people go, dengue goes. Find the places that spread it, and stop it for the fewest team-days.

A free app for barangay health workers and municipal health offices. Enter where residents spend the day and how many mosquitoes each barangay holds. BANTA-Y ranks hotspots by where transmission happens, plans vector control at least cost with a certified gap, traces cases from home addresses to places, and sounds an early alarm. It works without a signal.

Why place matters

Dengue is counted at home. It spreads where people spend the day.

Brigades and fogging follow the case map, and the case map follows home addresses. The mosquito does not.

A state of calamity in Albay

On September 10, 2026, the Albay provincial board declared a state of calamity over dengue: 2,179 cases and 14 deaths from January 1 to September 12, 84% more than the 1,183 cases of the same period in 2025. By September 21 the count had reached 2,888. Public schools closed from September 14 to 18 for cleanup and fogging, and hospitals in Naga City were full.

Daily Tribune, September 2026

Albay rose while the country fell

Across the Philippines, 192,112 dengue cases were recorded as of October 7, 2026, 24% fewer than the 254,137 of the same period in 2025.

Department of Health, via Daily Tribune, October 13, 2026

Bitten by day, close to the breeding site

Aedes aegypti bites by day and stays close to where it breeds, so the movement of people between houses drives dengue transmission. Cases are recorded by the patient's home address, but infection often happens where people spend the day: at school, at the market, at work.

Stoddard et al., 2013, PNAS

What the app does

Six screens, one job: send the brigades where transmission happens

Every screen runs on your phone. The pictures show the app with the synthetic test network D1.

1 · Network

Where people spend the day

List the barangays with their residents, mosquitoes per resident, share of the day spent away from home, and how strongly each place pulls visitors: schools, markets, jobs. Add positions or a table of travel minutes. BANTA-Y builds the residence-time matrix P: the share of the biting hours each barangay's residents spend in every place.

You enterResidents, mosquitoes per resident or a larval survey index, time away, attraction, travel times
You getThe matrix P as a heat map, the people present by day, and a flow map

2 · Transmission

One number for the network, and each place's share

BANTA-Y computes the reproduction number R0 of the whole network as the largest eigenvalue of a symmetric matrix, each barangay's local exposure number, the province-average R0 that hides the risk, and the share of transmission carried by each place. It ranks hotspots by how much one team-day of work there lowers R0.

You enterTransmission parameters (study values preset) and the season
You getR0, checked bounds, place shares, a hotspot ranking per team-day

3 · Vector control

The least-cost plan, with a certificate

Set a target such as R0 = 0.95, the team-days each barangay needs for full cleanup and larviciding, and the most mosquitoes that can be removed. BANTA-Y compares five plans and solves for the cheapest one exactly, with an interval that proves no plan can do better. A season slider shows how the bill grows in the rains.

You enterTarget R0, team-days per barangay, maximum reduction, season
You getTeam-days per barangay for five plans and a certified least cost

4 · Where people get infected

From home addresses back to places

Paste the cases by barangay of residence for a period. BANTA-Y works back to the infections by place with nonnegative least squares and reports an error bound that holds whenever residents spend more than half of the biting hours at home.

You enterCases by barangay of residence
You getInfections by place, the pressure per person present, and a ± bound

5 · Early warning

An alarm that adds up small rises

Paste weekly counts with the expected count of a normal year. The Poisson CUSUM adds up the excess week by week and sounds when it reaches a threshold. It comes with a guaranteed delay and an exact false-alarm rate.

You enterWeekly cases and the expected cases of a normal year
You getThe alarm week, a chart, the delay guarantee and the average run length

6 · DOH 4S

The 4S checklist

The Department of Health's 4S strategy: Search and destroy breeding sites, Self-protection, Seek early consultation, and Support fogging or spraying only in hotspots. BANTA-Y names the current hotspots next to the fourth S.

You enterTicks as your barangay acts
You getA checklist that stays on your device

Mathematics and Computational Sciences

How MCS comes into play

The mathematics proves what the app computes, the computing makes it run on a phone in a fraction of a second, and the sciences supply the mosquito, the virus and the movement of people.

MMathematics

Mathematics

A residence-time model links the mosquitoes in each barangay to the people who spend the day there. The next-generation method of van den Driessche and Watmough (2002) turns it into one number, and twelve propositions say what that number can and cannot do.

Movement becomes a matrix

Residents of barangay i stay home a share 1 − ωi of the day and visit place j in proportion to its attraction, less the farther it is.

Two generations of infection

Person to mosquito to person: M counts the new human cases one case causes, through mosquitoes, in each barangay.

R0 is a symmetric eigenvalue

Squaring makes the problem symmetric and positive semidefinite, so R02 is the largest eigenvalue of S, a sum of one rank-one term per place.

Bounds you can check by hand

Each barangay's local exposure number brackets R02 (Collatz–Wielandt), and the province average can only understate it.

Shares by place

The place shares add to 1, and each is the rate at which control at that place lowers R02: the hotspot ranking.

Least cost is a convex program

R02(u) is convex and never rises with control u, so the cheapest plan meeting a target r is a semidefinite program.

A quick plan that is always safe

If every local exposure number meets the target, the network does too. This linear program gives a safe plan at once.

A certified interval

Any positive semidefinite Z gives a lower bound on the least cost (weak duality), so the solver proves how close it is.

From homes to places

Rates by residence are a mix of pressures by place. When everyone stays home more than half of the day, P is invertible with a known error bound (Varah, 1975).

The alarm and its guarantees

The Poisson CUSUM with reference value kt for a θ-fold rise; with θ = 1.5, kt = 0.5 μt / ln 1.5.

Delay and false alarms

A rise of δ a week above kt is caught within ⌈h/δ⌉ weeks; the average run length is exact by a Markov chain (Brook and Evans, 1972).

The season scales everything

More mosquitoes in the rains multiply R02 by the same factor, so the cost of control can be planned ahead.

CComputing

Computing

All of it runs inside the app, written from scratch in JavaScript with no outside code, and checked against the Python of the study to about 10−11.

Jacobi eigen-solver

Rotations zero the off-diagonal of the symmetric matrix S until only its eigenvalues remain. The largest gives R02; its eigenvector gives the place shares.

Barrier interior-point method

Newton steps on a log-barrier, each solved with a Cholesky factorization, with a backtracking line search that keeps every point strictly feasible. On the test network it needs {{d1.exact_iterations}} Newton steps.

Simplex and least squares

A small dense two-phase simplex solves the linear-program certificate. Lawson–Hanson nonnegative least squares traces cases to places.

Fast enough for a province

In the study's Python timing, a network of 720 barangays, the size of Albay, gave R0 in {{timing.4.t_r0|f2}} s and the certified least-cost plan in about {{timing.4.t_exact|f0}} s. The test network D1 is solved in the app in a blink.

SSciences

Sciences

The model rests on what is known about Aedes aegypti and the dengue virus. Its parameters are declared design values within published ranges (Mordecai et al., 2017), and the app lets you change them.

The mosquito stays, people move

Aedes aegypti bites by day and stays close to where it breeds. Human movement between houses drives transmission (Stoddard et al., 2013). That is why BANTA-Y weighs mosquitoes by the people present by day.

Temperature sets the season

Dengue transmission by Aedes aegypti occurs between 18 and 34 °C and peaks around 26 to 29 °C (Mordecai et al., 2017). The season factor carries this into the plan.

The research

Read the study behind the app

Where People Go, Dengue Goes: A Verified Mobility-Coupled Network Model for Ranking Dengue Hotspots, Issuing Early Warnings, and Allocating Vector Control at Least Cost in Albay

Mathematics and Computational Sciences, Team (MCS-T)Legazpi City Science High School2026

Cases are logged at the patient's home address, but the day-biting Aedes aegypti mosquito infects people where they spend their daylight hours, so cleanup brigades and spraying are often sent to the wrong barangay. The study builds a model small enough for a laptop that links mosquito density in each barangay to the share of the day residents spend in every other barangay. Its reproduction number is the largest eigenvalue of a symmetric matrix, which yields bounds that flag where transmission can persist, a ranking of places by their share of transmission, a least-cost allocation of brigade team-days with a certified optimality gap, and an inversion from homes to places of infection. A Poisson CUSUM rule gives the outbreak alarm. A pilot with barangay dengue brigades, led by the Provincial Health Office, is recommended.

A planning aid, and your data stay with you

Not an order, not medical advice

BANTA-Y is a planning aid for health workers and local officials. It is not an official order and not medical advice. Decisions on vector control belong to the health office and the local government.

Anyone with fever should seek early consultation at the nearest health center.

Every number from the test network D1 on this page and in the app is synthetic: six made-up barangays used to check the method, not real Albay data. Real-world facts on this page are cited to their sources.

Privacy

The app computes everything on your device. The barangay data you enter are saved only in your browser's storage on that device, and you can export or clear them at any time. BANTA-Y has no account, no tracking and no analytics, and sends nothing anywhere.

This website loads its typefaces from Google Fonts. The app loads nothing from outside.
